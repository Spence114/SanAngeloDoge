Data centers/Skybox / Emergent campus

Data center · Inside city limits

Skybox / Emergent campus

A data center campus proposed on city-owned land at the old city farm, near US 67 North and City Farm Road. It is the only one of the three proposals inside city limits, so city zoning, noise, lighting, water, and sewer rules apply.

At a glance

Pink rows are developer projections, news-only items, or figures that differ across sources.

Developer
Skybox Datacenters of Dallas. Emergent markets the campus as SA1. Images of 380 agreement documents between the City and Emergent circulated online; the City says they were examples and nothing has been agreed.123
Buyer of record
San Angelo Data Center Campus Power LLC, managed by Skybox Partners IV LLC. Took over the buyer's interest Dec 19, 2025.3Title commitment GF No. 2505041TG, cited in our June report
Site
City-owned land northeast of town near US 67 North and City Farm Road. Annexed and rezoned from Ranch and Estate to Light Manufacturing.456
Acres and price
About 345 to 350 acres at $50,000 an acre: roughly $17.3 million to $17.5 million. Sources differ on the acreage.578News coverage
Land sale
Purchase and sale agreement signed in 2025. The City's page does not show the sale as closed.347
Size
Up to 600 MW in earlier project literature. Emergent's investor materials describe up to 1.2 GW. The developer's answers on the City page assume a first phase of 100 to 250 MW. Up to six buildings.2347Figures differ across sources
Power
AEP Texas, Red Creek 345-kV substation. The developer pays for a dedicated substation and upgrades. ERCOT sets the final load after its studies.49
Grid status
In ERCOT's first group of large requests ("Batch Zero"). On hold since the state audit ordered Aug 3, 2026.91011
Cooling and water
Closed loop, air cooled. The City will not supply water for evaporative cooling. Initial fill capped at 8.0 gallons per square foot of building; make-up water capped at 6% of loop volume a year. The developer's example: a 250,000 sq ft building needs about 2 million gallons to fill and about 60,000 gallons a year after. The ordinance would allow up to about 120,000 gallons a year for that building.412
Wastewater
The sewer ordinance sets rules and testing for discharges of treated cooling fluid to the city sewer. The developer says nothing goes to ground or storm drains and no PFAS will be added. The City has no written chemical list yet.413
Noise, light, setbacks
Sound study required, with a limit of 55 dBA day and night at the property line. Shielded lighting with a 90 degree cutoff, 3000K at most. Buildings at least 300 feet from residential zoning and from homes outside city limits; ground equipment at least 200 feet away. 75 feet and three stories at most.14
Jobs
Developer projection: 30 to 60 in year one, 120 to 200 at full build over 10 years, average pay $75,000 to $110,000.4Developer figures
Local incentives
The City says no incentives or subsidies have been offered. A Chapter 380 agreement is to be proposed and discussed in open session. No date set.14
State tax break
The state sales tax exemption on equipment and electricity applies to certified data centers that invest at least $200 million and create 20 jobs paying at least 120% of the county average wage. News coverage says this project is likely to qualify.1516
Economic study
Under way with Cirrus Advisors. No figures published. Records reviewed for our June report show Cirrus was introduced to the City by Emergent's CEO.34
Tenant
None named.4
Independent water review
No independent water impact study or environmental review has been done, and the City says no environmental review is currently planned.4
State standards
The Governor announced Aug 7, 2026 that Skybox committed to comply with his data center standards.17
School district
Not confirmed. The Cipher site next door is reported to be in Veribest ISD.9News coverage
