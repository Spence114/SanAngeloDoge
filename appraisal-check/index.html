Appraisal check

Is your property value fair?

Every tax bill starts with the value the Appraisal District sets. If it is too high, you pay too much to the City, the County, and the schools, every year. Appraisal Check compares your value with similar properties nearby and tells you, in plain words, whether a protest is worth your time, before the deadline.1

Get early accessWhat it does

Coming in 2027 Built from the Appraisal District's public roll.

For anyone who pays a property tax bill

Homeowners

Find out in minutes whether your value is out of line, and check that your homestead and other exemptions are on file.

Rental owners

Check every rental at once. A lower value lowers the tax you would otherwise pass on in rent.

Businesses

Compare your building and land with similar properties, and get reminded before the April 15 rendition for business property.

Land and ranch owners

See how your land is valued against nearby parcels, and whether it carries the special use valuation it should.

What it does

  • Neighborhood comparison

    Your value per square foot next to similar properties nearby, drawn from the Appraisal District's public roll.

  • Value history

    How your value has changed year by year, and how that compares with your neighborhood.

  • Exemption check

    Flags exemptions you may qualify for but are not receiving, such as the homestead or over-65 exemption.

  • Deadline reminders

    Protest by May 15, or 30 days after your notice arrives, whichever is later. Business property renditions are due April 15.12

  • A report you can bring

    A printable summary of the comparison to take to an informal meeting or your protest hearing.

  • What it means for your bill

    The tax each government charges on your value, using this year's adopted rates.

Questions

Will you file my protest for me?

No. We give you the comparison and the report; you file and present your own protest. In Texas, representing someone in a protest for pay requires registration as a property tax consultant.3

Where does the data come from?

The Appraisal District's public appraisal roll. Texas does not require sale prices to be disclosed, so comparisons use appraised values and property details, not private sale prices.

Is this from the Appraisal District?

No. San Angelo DOGE is independent. We are not affiliated with the Appraisal District or any taxing unit.

When will it be ready?

We plan to launch before the 2027 appraisal notices go out. Sign up and you will be first to know.
