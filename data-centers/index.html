What a data center is

A data center is a large industrial building full of computers. Those computers run websites, store files, and, more and more, train and run artificial intelligence. The owner sells space, electricity, and cooling to technology companies.14

The two big inputs are electricity and a way to get rid of heat. Heat leaves through air coolers, which use little water, or through evaporating water, which uses a lot.14 For a community, the questions are the same as for any big industrial plant: who pays for the roads and power lines, how much water it uses, how loud it is, what it pays in taxes, and what happens if it fails.

When a data center helps a community, and when it costs one

The same building can be either. The terms decide which.

Signs it can work

  • It sits inside city limits, where zoning, noise, lighting, and water rules apply.
  • It cools with a closed loop and air, and buys no drinking water to evaporate.
  • The developer pays for its own substation, power lines, and road wear.
  • A named tenant has signed a lease before public land or tax breaks change hands.
  • Tax breaks are small or none, and any deal claws money back if jobs or investment fall short.
  • Its new taxable value lands where it actually helps local budgets.
  • A bond or deposit pays for removal if it shuts down.
  • Owners and investors are named, and agreements are public before the vote.

Signs it will cost us

  • It sits on county land, where no local zoning, noise, or water rules apply.
  • It burns natural gas on site around the clock near homes, creeks, or schools.
  • It draws groundwater or city water for evaporative cooling during drought.
  • No tenant, no named investors, and financing that depends on future AI demand.
  • Large tax breaks, unpublished agreements, or confidentiality deals with officials.
  • Grid or transmission costs land on other ratepayers.
  • Ownership can change hands with no local review.
  • The only public meeting happens after the permits are filed.

These tests are our analysis, drawn from our June 2026 report.4

Questions to bring to any data center proposal

Use these at City Council, Commissioners Court, or a community meeting.

  1. Who is the tenant, and is the lease signed?
  2. Where does the water come from, and how many gallons a year, in writing?
  3. Who pays for the substation, power lines, and road wear?
  4. What tax breaks are requested, from which governments, and what is clawed back if promises are missed?
  5. How loud will it be at the nearest home, in dBA and dBC, day and night?
  6. Who owns it, and can ownership change without notice?
  7. What bond covers cleanup and removal if it closes?
